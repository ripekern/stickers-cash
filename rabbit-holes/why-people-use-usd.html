Why People Use USD

If Bitcoin Cash and other alternatives exist, why do people feel compelled to use Federal Reserve Notes?

The United States dollar is not the safest form of money. It is not the most private. It is not the most durable. It is not even the most widely accepted — Bitcoin Cash can be sent anywhere in the world, at any time, without a bank, without permission, and without an intermediary.

So why do people still use it?

Compulsion, not choice

The answer is not convenience. It is compulsion. The system is designed so that using the USD is not optional — it is mandatory. Taxes must be paid in Federal Reserve Notes. Wages are deposited in dollars. Rent is denominated in dollars. The entire economic infrastructure — banking, credit, insurance, legal contracts — operates exclusively in USD.

This is not a free market. A free market would allow people to choose whatever money they find most useful, most stable, most private. But the government has made its money the only money that counts for legal purposes. You can use Bitcoin Cash, but you still owe your taxes in dollars. You can price your goods in BCH, but the IRS will value them in USD when they come to collect.

The legal monopoly on money

Legal tender laws do not force merchants to accept cash. But they force everyone to settle their debts with the government in Federal Reserve Notes. This creates a baseline demand for USD that has nothing to do with its quality as money and everything to do with its status as the government's preferred instrument.

When the government demands taxes in a specific currency, it creates an artificial demand for that currency. Everyone needs it to stay out of trouble. Everyone needs it to interact with the state. And because everyone needs it, everyone uses it — even if they would prefer something else.

The illusion of choice

People believe they choose to use dollars. They do not. They use dollars because the system leaves them no real alternative. You can try to live entirely in Bitcoin Cash, but you will still need dollars to pay your property taxes, your income taxes, your traffic tickets, and your court fees. The moment you interact with the state — and the state is everywhere — you need dollars.

This is not a market failure. It is a market suppression. The government has used its monopoly on force to suppress competing currencies. It has made it illegal to circulate private banknotes. It has regulated exchanges into compliance. It has made it difficult, expensive, and sometimes illegal to use alternative money.

The compulsion is the point

The reason people use USD is not because it is good money. It is because they have no choice. And the absence of choice is the entire point. If people could freely choose their money, they would choose money that cannot be inflated, cannot be tracked, and cannot be controlled. They would choose Bitcoin Cash.

But as long as the government can compel the use of its currency — through taxes, through regulation, through the threat of force — it will maintain its monopoly. The USD is not popular because it is good. It is popular because it is mandatory.

Living a lie

The truth is that pretty much everyone really is not a public officer. They do not reside in the District of Columbia. They are free men and women. But they are living a lie. They carry Social Security Numbers. They accept wages in Federal Reserve Notes. They file tax returns. Every action reinforces the presumption that they are government officers operating within the federal jurisdiction.

They are not officers. They are private citizens who have been conditioned to accept the presumption. And the system depends on that conditioning. As long as people keep using the script, keep carrying the numbers, keep filing the returns, the system can continue to treat them as subjects rather than sovereign individuals.

There is a verse in the King James Bible that says no man might buy or sell save he that had the mark, or the name of the beast, or the number of his name. Federal Reserve Notes look a lot like that mark. You cannot participate in the economy without them — or at least, not without great difficulty. You need a bank account. You need a Social Security Number. You need to be in the system. The system has made its currency the only practical way to buy and sell.

The exit is here

Now that true money is here in the form of Bitcoin Cash, they can separate themselves from these false systems and no longer have to pretend. If they only transact in BCH, then they will no longer be assumed to be public officers. If they can get a job without being demanded of a Social Security Number, then they will be able to not be assumed to be a public officer.

This is not theory. This is practical. Every transaction in BCH is a step out of the presumption. Every job that pays in BCH or accepts payment in BCH is a job that does not require you to carry the government's number. Every merchant who accepts BCH is a merchant who does not need to verify your status as a government officer.

There really is a lot of hope here. The exit is not theoretical. It is not somewhere in the future. It is here, now, for anyone who is willing to use it. The only question is whether enough people will choose to walk through the door.

Breaking the compulsion

The only way to break the compulsion is to build alternatives that cannot be suppressed. Bitcoin Cash is that alternative. It is censorship-resistant, inflation-proof, and globally accessible. It does not require a bank. It does not require permission. It does not require the state.

Every transaction made in Bitcoin Cash is a step away from compulsion. Every person who learns to use it is a person who no longer needs the state's money. And every merchant who accepts it is a merchant who is no longer dependent on the government's currency.

The compulsion ends when the alternative becomes the norm. That is what this site is about.
