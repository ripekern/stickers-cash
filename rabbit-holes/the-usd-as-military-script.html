The USD as Military Script

Federal Reserve Notes share characteristics with military script — currency issued for government use. What happens if you're using government currency?

Federal Reserve Notes are not dollars. The Constitution prohibits states from making anything but gold and silver coin a tender in payment of debts. Federal Reserve Notes are technically liabilities of the Federal Reserve, not the Treasury. They were originally intended for government transactions — paying soldiers, government contractors, and managing federal operations.

Military script is currency issued by a military authority for use in specific contexts, typically occupied territories or military installations. It is not intended for general circulation among the civilian population. Historical examples include Allied Military Marks in occupied Germany, Japanese invasion money in Southeast Asia, and U.S. military payment certificates used to pay troops abroad.

The claim is that Federal Reserve Notes function similarly. They were created to manage government operations, not to serve as the general currency of the people. If this is true, then using Federal Reserve Notes raises questions about your relationship to the government.

The first question is about the codes. The statutes that designate Federal Reserve Notes as legal tender apply to officers and agents of the federal government, not to private citizens in their private dealings. What the codes actually say walks through this distinction and what it means for the presumption of applicability.

If you are using government script, does that mean you are operating as a government officer? The structural argument runs like this: military script is issued for military administration, and the people using it are, by definition, operating in a government capacity. Are you a government officer? examines the logic — whether you signed anything or not, the system presumes you are inside its jurisdiction.

Then there is the question of what "the United States" actually is. 26 U.S.C. § 7701(a)(10) defines the United States as the District of Columbia — a municipal corporation, not the republic most people think they live in. Your Social Security Number, assigned at birth and owned by the government, becomes further evidence of public officer status.

Despite all this, most people keep using USD. The reason is compulsion, not choice — the system is designed so that using Federal Reserve Notes is mandatory, not optional. And the connection to Bitcoin Cash is straightforward: it is money that does not require you to be anyone's agent.
