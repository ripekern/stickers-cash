Are You Being Compelled to Be a Government Agent?

Social security numbers as evidence of being a public officer. Are you being compelled to be an agent of the government?

You were assigned a number at birth. Or shortly after. Your parents applied for it. Or the hospital did it for them. You did not choose it. You did not consent to it. But it has followed you your entire life: your Social Security Number.

What is this number? The government calls it an identifier for tax purposes. But look closer at what it actually represents.

The SSN as evidence of agency

A Social Security Number is not just a number. It is evidence that you are registered in the federal system. It is evidence that you have been assigned a role. It is evidence that you are a participant in the government's administrative framework.

The Social Security Act (42 U.S.C. § 405) establishes the system. But it does not compel you to participate — at least, not explicitly. The compulsion comes from everywhere else: employers require it for payroll. Banks require it for accounts. The IRS requires it for tax returns. States require it for driver's licenses. Credit bureaus require it for credit reports.

You cannot function in modern society without one. And once you have one, you are inside the system. The number is your identifier. It tracks your earnings. It tracks your benefits. It tracks your taxes. It identifies you as a participant in the federal administrative state.

The public officer presumption

Here is the critical question: if you have a Social Security Number, are you a public officer?

The argument runs as follows:

  • Social Security is a federal program for government employees and participants.
  • By accepting a Social Security Number, you are enrolling in a federal program.
  • Enrollment in a federal program is evidence that you are acting in a federal capacity.
  • Therefore, by accepting a Social Security Number, you are presumed to be a public officer or federal employee — whether you know it or not.

This is not a conspiracy theory. It is a structural observation. The Social Security system was originally created for federal employees. It was later expanded to include private employees. But the system is still a federal system. And by participating in it, you are accepting a federal role.

Property of the United States

Your Social Security Number is not your property. It is property of the United States government. You do not own it. You cannot sell it. You cannot transfer it. You cannot change it (except in rare circumstances). It is assigned to you by the government, and it remains the government's property.

This is not a minor detail. It means that the identifier the government uses to track you — the identifier on your tax returns, your bank accounts, your employment records, your credit reports — is not yours. It belongs to the government. You are using the government's property to identify yourself within the government's system.

The compulsion is the point

You did not choose to have a Social Security Number. You were assigned one. And once you had it, you could not function without it. The system is designed so that you must use it. The compulsion is not accidental — it is intentional. The government wants you inside the system. It wants you using its identifiers. It wants you participating in its programs.

Because once you are inside, you are subject to the codes. Once you are inside, you are presumed to be a federal actor. Once you are inside, the administrative jurisdiction applies to you.

Rebutting the presumption

The presumption can be rebutted. But it is difficult. You would need to demonstrate that you are not participating in the federal system — that you are not using federal identifiers, not receiving federal benefits, not operating under federal licenses. You would need to live outside the system.

This is nearly impossible in modern society. You need a bank account. You need a job. You need to pay taxes. You need to drive. And all of these require a Social Security Number.

So you are compelled. Not by force, but by necessity. The system has made it so that you cannot function without participating. And by participating, you are presumed to be a government agent.
