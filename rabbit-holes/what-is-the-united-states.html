What Is the United States?

26 U.S.C. § 7701(a)(10) defines the United States as the District of Columbia. What does that mean for you?

When you say "I live in the United States," what do you mean? The geographic landmass? The republic? Or something else entirely?

The Internal Revenue Code has an answer. Under 26 U.S.C. § 7701(a)(10), the term "United States" is defined specifically:

"The term 'United States' when used in a geographical sense means only the States and the District of Columbia."

This is not a definition of the country. It is a definition of a jurisdiction. And notice what it includes: the District of Columbia.

The District of Columbia is not a state

The District of Columbia is a federal district — a 10-mile square created by the Constitution (Article I, Section 8) to serve as the seat of the federal government. It is not a state. It is not part of any state. It is a federal enclave under the exclusive jurisdiction of Congress.

When the code defines "United States" as "the States and the District of Columbia," it is defining the United States as a federal jurisdiction — not as the geographic territory or the sovereign nation.

The definition of "State"

The code also defines "State" in 26 U.S.C. § 7701(a)(10):

"The term 'State' shall be construed to include the District of Columbia, where such construction is necessary to carry out provisions of this title."

The District of Columbia can be construed as a "State" for tax purposes. This is not a typo. It is a legal fiction — a construction that allows the federal government to apply its tax code to the District and, by extension, to anyone operating within the federal jurisdiction.

What this means for you

If you think you are a citizen of the United States — the country, the republic, the landmass — you are operating under a misunderstanding. The code does not recognize that entity. It recognizes only the federal jurisdiction: the District of Columbia and the States (which, for tax purposes, includes the District).

This is why tax law is confusing. You are being taxed as if you are operating within the federal jurisdiction, even if you are a private citizen living in a state like Texas, California, or New York. The code presumes you are a federal actor because you are using federal script, federal identifiers, and federal licenses.

The implication

If the United States, for purposes of the code, is the District of Columbia, then the "United States" is a corporation — a municipal corporation created by Congress in 1871. This is not a conspiracy theory. It is a legal fact. The District of Columbia is a municipal corporation, and the code defines the United States as that corporation.

This raises a question: are you a citizen of the republic, or are you a citizen of the corporation? The answer determines which law applies to you — constitutional law or commercial law.
