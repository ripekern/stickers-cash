Are You a Government Officer?

If you are using military script, does that mean you are operating as an agent of the government?

Military script is issued for military administration — to pay troops, purchase supplies, and manage occupied economies. It is not intended for civilian circulation. If Federal Reserve Notes are military script, then using them places you inside the government's administrative framework, not outside of it.

The logic of the claim

The argument runs as follows:

  • Military script is issued to military personnel and government contractors for official use.
  • Civilians in the issuing country do not use it — they use the sovereign currency.
  • If Federal Reserve Notes are military script, then the people using them are, by definition, operating in a government capacity.
  • Therefore, anyone using Federal Reserve Notes is either a government officer, agent, or employee — whether they know it or not.

This is not a theory about what the law says. It is a structural observation: the tool defines the role. The currency you use reveals the capacity in which you are operating.

What "officer" means in this context

An officer of the United States is not necessarily a politician or a general. Under federal law, an officer is anyone who exercises authority derived from the United States. This includes:

  • Federal employees and civil servants
  • Members of the armed forces
  • Licensed professionals (doctors, lawyers, contractors) operating under federal or state license
  • Recipients of federal benefits (Social Security, Medicare, unemployment)
  • Holders of federal identifiers (Social Security numbers, Employer Identification Numbers)
  • Users of federal script (Federal Reserve Notes)

The last category is the most consequential. If the script defines the role, then using the script makes you an officer — regardless of your intent or understanding.

Presumption, not consent

You did not sign a contract to become a government officer. You did not raise your right hand and swear an oath. But you accepted the script. You used the number. You filed the return. The system operates on presumption: if you are using the tools, you are playing the role. The burden of rebuttal falls on you.

This is how administrative systems work. They do not require your explicit consent. They require your participation. And participation is defined by the tools you use and the identifiers you carry.

The question this raises

If you are using military script, are you operating as a private citizen — or as a government officer who has not yet recognized their own status? The answer determines which jurisdiction applies to you: the common law (for private citizens) or the administrative code (for government officers).

This is not a question most people have been taught to ask. But it is the question that the monetary system forces you to answer — every time you use a Federal Reserve Note.
