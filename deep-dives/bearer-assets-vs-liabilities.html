Bearer assets vs liabilities

The fundamental difference between digital dollars and Bitcoin Cash. Who holds the claim, who can freeze it, and what it means for ownership.

The core difference

Bitcoin — specifically Bitcoin Cash (BCH) — is cash rebuilt for the internet so you can get off the ice. It works just like handing someone a bill, but online. You hold it yourself in your own wallet, like cash in your pocket. You send it directly peer-to-peer to anyone, anywhere, in seconds. No bank, no Visa, no PayPal in the middle to watch, charge, block, or reverse it.

The comparison table

Question Digital dollar Bitcoin Cash
Who holds it? A bank or payment app holds the balance. You hold a promise. You hold the key. The coin is on a public ledger.
Who can create more? The central bank and commercial banks, within policy and capital rules. No one. The 21 million supply cap is part of the protocol.
Who can freeze it? The institution, platform, or government that controls the account. No bank, platform, or government can freeze the coin without your keys.
Who pays for expansion? Everyone holding the currency, through higher prices. No one. The supply does not expand.
How does payment settle? Through banks and processors, with reversibility and fees. Peer-to-peer in seconds, final on-chain, for less than a cent.

Promises vs property

That is the difference between a promise and a bearer asset. A dollar in a bank is a liability of the bank. A BCH coin is not anyone's liability. It is a digital object that you control with a key, and the network checks the signature without asking a bank for permission.

Why banks oppose bitcoin

Banks create money when they lend. They charge fees on every transaction. They can freeze accounts, reverse payments, and expand the supply at will. Their ledgers are private — you cannot see where the money goes or who benefits. Bitcoin Cash removes their power to do any of this. That is why the banking system fights against it, regulates it, and tries to control it. The real choice is between private ledgers controlled by a few and a public ledger auditable by everyone.

The exit from the banking system

Cash gave people an exit from the banking system. You could keep a week's wages in your pocket and no one could freeze it. BCH gives the internet an exit. If you hold the keys, no bank can lend out your balance, no processor can reverse your payment, and no committee can expand the supply.

The property right

That is the whole promise: not a different app, but a different property right. You own the money directly, the way you own a dollar bill.

The fundamental shift

With digital dollars, you have a claim against an institution. With Bitcoin Cash, you have direct ownership of a digital object. The institution can fail, freeze, or reverse. The blockchain cannot — as long as you hold the keys.
