Deep Dive · Chapter 3

The satoshi mindset

The land-plot analogy and how to shift from thinking in dollars to thinking in coins. Why focusing on satoshis, not price, is the key to long-term thinking.

The land-plot analogy

Imagine you buy 10 acres of land in a small town. Over the next five years, the local government prints enormous amounts of paper currency. The dollar price of land moves with the market. Does the physical size of your land change? No. You still own exactly 10 acres of Earth. Your share of the town's total land is untouched.

Bitcoin Cash works the same way. If you own 1,000,000,000 satoshis (10 coins), you own 1/2,100,000th of the entire 21-million-coin supply. No matter what the Federal Reserve does, no matter how much M2 expands, no matter what the daily market says, those satoshis are an immutable, un-dilutable share of a finite economic territory.

The mindset comparison

The variable fiat mindset The fixed hard-asset mindset
"I have $5,000 in the bank. I feel safe today, but I know this money will buy fewer groceries and fewer home repairs every year." "I own 1,000,000,000 satoshis. I know exactly what I have. Nobody can print more to shrink my share. My wealth is permanent, un-duplicable digital cash — ready to circulate the moment I choose to spend it."

How to keep the long view

  1. Focus on circulation, not price. Don't stare at the daily dollar chart. Look at how your coins move. The point isn't to hoard satoshis — it's to keep them flowing: work for them, spend them at local shops, pass them to neighbors, family, friends.
  2. Look through the daily quote. The dollar price is an exchange rate, and it changes as the market values each unit. Your satoshi count does not.
  3. Remember you can spend fractions. If you need $200 for a repair, you don't sell all your coins. You spend a small fraction — maybe 40,000,000 satoshis — straight from your wallet, and the rest of your stack stays in circulation, ready for the next local transaction.

The psychology of scarcity

When you think in satoshis, you're thinking in terms of a fixed, finite resource. There are only 21 million BCH, and each BCH is divisible into 100 million satoshis. That's 2.1 quadrillion satoshis total. Every satoshi you own is a permanent, un-dilutable share of that total.

This is fundamentally different from thinking in dollars, where the supply is constantly expanding. When you think in dollars, you're constantly losing ground. When you think in satoshis, you're holding your ground.

Why the dollar price is misleading

The dollar price of BCH is just an exchange rate. It tells you how many dollars one BCH can buy at a given moment. But it doesn't tell you anything about the value of BCH itself. The value of BCH is determined by its utility as a payment network and its scarcity as a fixed-supply asset.

When the dollar price goes up, it could mean:

  • BCH is becoming more valuable (more people want to use it)
  • The dollar is becoming less valuable (more dollars are being printed)
  • Both

When the dollar price goes down, it could mean:

  • BCH is becoming less valuable (fewer people want to use it)
  • The dollar is becoming more valuable (fewer dollars are being printed)
  • Both

The point is: the dollar price is a noisy signal. Your satoshi count is a clear signal. Focus on the clear signal.

The long game

Framed this way, saving becomes what it should have been all along: keeping permanent, un-duplicable digital cash flowing locally. The goal is not to time the market or chase short-term gains. The goal is to keep satoshis moving — from your hands to the next local transaction, and the next.

This is the satoshi mindset. It's the mindset of a saver, not a speculator. It's the mindset of someone who understands that true wealth is not measured in dollars, but in ownership of scarce, useful assets.

And it's the mindset that allows you to sleep well at night, knowing that no matter what happens to the dollar, your satoshis will still be there, still be yours, and still be spendable.
