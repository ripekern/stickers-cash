The receipts: primary sources for the four leaks

Don't take my word for it. These are primary sources, not a YouTube video. Read them yourself.

This deep dive expands on the sources mentioned in "Four leaks in every digital dollar." Each claim in that post is backed by verifiable documentation from the Federal Reserve, Bank of England, and other authoritative sources.

The evidence

ClaimSourceWhat it shows
A central bank was created in 1913 after a secret bankers' meeting. Federal Reserve History: Jekyll Island; Federal Reserve Act Signed into Law Six men drafted the plan; the final law closely resembled it.
The Fed is independent and sets monetary policy. Fed: Who We Are; Fed: Independent within the government Seven governors serve 14-year terms; the FOMC sets policy.
The dollar became fiat in 1971. Federal Reserve History: Gold Convertibility Ends Nixon suspended gold redemption; the system "turned into a fiat one."
Banks create deposits when they lend. Bank of England: Money creation in the modern economy "Whenever a bank makes a loan, it simultaneously creates a matching deposit..."
Most money is digital. Fed: Money and Payments "Commercial bank money is the digital form of money that is most commonly used by the public."
The Fed publishes aggregate balance sheet totals — but not who benefits. FRED: M2SL; FRED: WALCL M2 was $23.34T in Aug 2026; Fed assets were $6.76T in Oct 2026. The totals are public. The counterparties, the beneficiaries, and the allocation of new money remain hidden.

Data retrieved October 22, 2026. M2 is the seasonally adjusted M2SL series; Fed assets are the WALCL series. For the price level over the same period, see FRED: CPIAUCSL.

Why the sources matter

These aren't opinions or interpretations. They're official statements from the institutions themselves. The Federal Reserve acknowledges its independence and policy-setting role. The Bank of England explains that banks create money through lending. The data is publicly available, even if the details about who benefits most are not.

Understanding these sources is essential for anyone trying to grasp why the current monetary system works the way it does. The four leaks in every digital dollar — the watched, freezable, fee-taxed, and melting nature of digital money — aren't bugs. They're features of a system designed this way from the beginning.

For more context on how this system developed, see The USD as military script, The Federal Reserve: history and structure, and How the dollar became digital.
