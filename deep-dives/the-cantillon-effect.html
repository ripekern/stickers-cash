The Cantillon Effect

New money does not arrive evenly. It enters through the banking system and financial markets. The banks, bond sellers, and borrowers closest to the creation point can spend it before prices fully adjust. Everyone else meets it later as higher prices.

This is the part that turns a technical fact into a moral question. If money is created by banks and central banks, then the first users get an advantage that savers and wage earners do not. You just have to follow the ledger.

Richard Cantillon's insight

The effect is named after Richard Cantillon, an 18th-century Irish-French economist who observed this pattern in the 1750s. Cantillon watched what happened when Spanish gold arrived in Europe. The gold did not raise all prices at once. It flowed through specific channels—first to the king, then to his creditors, then to the broader economy. Each group spent the new money before prices had fully adjusted.

Cantillon wrote:

"The money which enters into a State by successive augmentations does not raise the prices of commodities in the same proportion."

— Richard Cantillon, Essai sur la Nature du Commerce en Général (1755)

This is not a theory. It is an observation of how money actually moves through an economy. The first recipients get to buy goods and assets at old prices. By the time the money reaches workers and savers, prices have risen. The purchasing power has been transferred from late recipients to early recipients. This pattern has repeated every time central banks create new money, from the Spanish Empire to the Federal Reserve.

How it works in practice

When the Fed creates reserves, those reserves go to banks. Banks lend to their best customers—large corporations, real estate developers, financial institutions. These borrowers buy assets: stocks, bonds, real estate. Asset prices rise. The people who own those assets get richer.

Then the money trickles down. Companies hire more workers. Wages rise. But by the time workers get the new money, the price of housing, food, and energy has already increased. The worker's raise does not keep up with the cost of living. The transfer has already happened.

Consider what happened after 2008. The Fed created $4 trillion in reserves. The S&P 500 tripled. Housing prices doubled in major cities. The people who owned stocks and real estate saw their wealth explode. The people who held savings in bank accounts saw their purchasing power evaporate. The Cantillon effect is not abstract. It is the mechanism that separates the asset-owning class from everyone else.

The rich ruleth over the poor, and the borrower [is] servant to the lender.

— Proverbs 22:7

Why it matters

The Cantillon effect explains why asset owners get richer while wage earners fall behind. It explains why the stock market can hit record highs while main street struggles. It is not a bug in the system. It is a feature of how money is created.

The Fed's own research confirms this. A 2019 paper from the Federal Reserve Bank of New York found that "monetary policy has different effects on different households." The paper showed that expansionary monetary policy benefits households with more assets and hurts households with fewer assets. The central bank that is supposed to promote "maximum employment" and "stable prices" is actually transferring wealth from savers to speculators, from workers to asset owners.

This is why Bitcoin Cash matters. BCH has a fixed supply. No central bank can create new coins. No bank can create deposits out of thin air. The money supply is predictable. The Cantillon effect cannot happen because there is no new money being injected at specific points in the network. Every holder of BCH owns an immutable share of a fixed supply. No one gets new coins first. No one gets an advantage. The ledger is public, the supply is transparent, and the rules are enforced by mathematics, not by committees meeting behind closed doors.

A moral question

This is where economics meets ethics. The Cantillon effect is not just a technical observation. It is a system that transfers wealth from the patient to the privileged, from the righteous to the wicked.

Consider what you are being asked to accept. When the Fed creates money, it does not go to build hospitals, schools, or roads. It goes to financial markets. It goes to the people who already have access to credit, to the banks that already have relationships with the central bank, to the speculators who know how to play the game. The money that is supposed to be "for the people" flows through channels that benefit the people who least need it.

And you have no choice. You cannot opt out of the dollar system. You are forced to hold dollars, to save in dollars, to be paid in dollars. You are required to participate in a system that devalues your labor, your savings, your time. Every time the Fed creates money, your dollars buy less. That is not a market outcome. That is a transfer of purchasing power from you to them.

A false balance [is] abomination to the LORD: but a just weight [is] his delight.

— Proverbs 11:1

The Bible has something to say about this. The Old Testament is full of warnings against unjust weights and measures—against the practice of debasing currency, of using dishonest scales to transfer wealth from the honest to the powerful. A false balance is not just a technical error. It is an abomination. It is a violation of the most basic principles of justice.

Divers weights [are] an abomination unto the LORD; and a false balance [is] not good.

— Proverbs 20:23

When the government creates money out of thin air and spends it into the economy, it is using a false balance. It is taking purchasing power from everyone who holds dollars and giving it to the people who get the new money first. It is debasing the currency in a way that benefits the wicked and punishes the righteous.

The wicked in this case are not abstract. They are the banks that get the new reserves. They are the corporations that borrow at negative real interest rates. They are the speculators who buy assets with cheap money and watch them appreciate. They are the people who understand the system and use it to enrich themselves.

But getting first access to the new money is not just about understanding the system. It is about being part of the system. You cannot simply walk up to the Fed window and borrow at the discount rate. You have to be licensed. You have to be registered. You have to have identifying numbers. You have to be a member of the clubs that matter. And increasingly, you have to compromise your faith to play the game.

The financial system is not neutral. It requires participation in structures that conflict with Biblical principles. It demands allegiance to institutions that operate in secret. It rewards those who join the right societies, who attend the right schools, who sit on the right boards. It punishes those who refuse to identify themselves with numbers, who refuse to submit to licensing, who refuse to compromise their convictions to get a seat at the table.

Be ye not unequally yoked together with unbelievers: for what fellowship hath righteousness with unrighteousness? and what communion hath light with darkness?… Wherefore come out from among them, and be ye separate, saith the Lord, and touch not the unclean [thing]; and I will receive you.

— 2 Corinthians 6:14, 17

This is the choice that the Cantillon system forces on you. You can either be righteous and stay outside the system, or you can compromise your faith to get inside and get your share of the new money. You cannot do both. The system is designed to punish those who refuse to participate. And the punishment is not just social or professional. It is monetary. It is the slow erosion of your purchasing power, the steady transfer of your labor to people who were willing to play the game.

The righteous are the people who play by the rules. The people who work, save, and hold their money in the bank. The people who believe that if they are patient and responsible, their money will hold its value. The people who do not understand the financial system and are punished for their honesty.

Wealth gotten by vanity shall be diminished: but he that gathereth by labour shall increase.

— Proverbs 13:11

This is the heart of the Cantillon effect. Wealth created by money printing is wealth gotten by vanity. It is not earned. It is not deserved. It is taken from the people who produce and given to the people who manipulate. And the Bible is clear: wealth gotten by vanity shall be diminished. It may last for a season, but it does not endure.

Go to now, ye rich men, weep and howl for your miseries that shall come upon you. Your riches are corrupted, and your garments are motheaten. Your gold and silver is cankered; and the rust of them shall be a witness against you, and shall eat your flesh as it were fire. Ye have heaped treasure together for the last days.

— James 5:1-3

When James writes about the rich, he is not talking about people who produce value. He is talking about people who hoard, who exploit, who use their position to extract wealth from others. He is talking about the Cantillon class—the people who get the new money first and use it to buy up assets while everyone else's purchasing power evaporates.

The Cantillon effect is not just an economic phenomenon. It is a moral failure. It is a system that punishes virtue and rewards vice. It is a system that takes from the righteous through devaluation and gives to the wicked through privileged access.

This is why Bitcoin Cash matters. BCH is a just weight. It is a balance that cannot be debased. The supply is fixed. The rules are transparent. No one gets special access. No one gets new coins first. The wealth you accumulate through labor is wealth that cannot be taken from you through monetary manipulation. It is a return to honest weights, to just balances, to a system where wealth is gathered by labor, not gotten by vanity.

The key insight

The Cantillon effect is about structure. When money is created by banks and central banks, the first recipients get an advantage. When money has a fixed supply, that advantage disappears. Bitcoin Cash eliminates the Cantillon effect by making the supply predictable and the rules transparent. No one gets new coins first. No one gets an advantage. That is one of the reasons Bitcoin Cash exists.

The receipts

Cantillon's insight was published in 1755 in his Essai sur la Nature du Commerce en Général. Modern economists have documented the effect empirically. The Fed's own research shows that monetary policy affects different groups differently.

  • Cantillon (1755): "The money which enters into a State by successive augmentations does not raise the prices of commodities in the same proportion."
  • Federal Reserve Bank of New York (2019): "Monetary policy has different effects on different households. Expansionary monetary policy benefits households with more assets."
  • European Central Bank (2020): "Monetary policy can have distributional consequences across households with different characteristics, including their position in the wealth distribution."
  • Bank of England (2012): "The increase in asset prices resulting from quantitative easing has disproportionately benefited the wealthiest households."

  • James 5:1-3: "Go to now, ye rich men, weep and howl for your miseries that shall come upon you. Your riches are corrupted… Your gold and silver is cankered."
  • 2 Corinthians 6:14, 17: "Be ye not unequally yoked together with unbelievers… Wherefore come out from among them, and be ye separate, saith the Lord."

The pattern is real, whether you call it the Cantillon effect or not. Every time central banks create money, the first recipients win. Every time. The question is whether you want to be one of the winners or one of the losers. Bitcoin Cash is the exit for people who refuse to play that game.
