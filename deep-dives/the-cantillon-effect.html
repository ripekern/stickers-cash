The Cantillon Effect

New money does not arrive evenly. It enters through the banking system and financial markets. The banks, bond sellers, and borrowers closest to the creation point can spend it before prices fully adjust. Everyone else meets it later as higher prices.

This is the part that turns a technical fact into a moral question. If money is created by banks and central banks, then the first users get an advantage that savers and wage earners do not. You do not have to be a conspiracy theorist to see it. You just have to follow the ledger.

Richard Cantillon's insight

The effect is named after Richard Cantillon, an 18th-century Irish-French economist who observed that when new money enters an economy, it does not raise all prices at once. The money flows through specific channels—first to the banks, then to their best customers, then to the broader economy. Each group spends the new money before prices have fully adjusted.

The first recipients get to buy goods and assets at old prices. By the time the money reaches workers and savers, prices have risen. The purchasing power has been transferred from late recipients to early recipients. This is not a theory. It is an observable pattern that has repeated every time central banks create new money.

How it works in practice

When the Fed creates reserves, those reserves go to banks. Banks lend to their best customers—large corporations, real estate developers, financial institutions. These borrowers buy assets: stocks, bonds, real estate. Asset prices rise. The people who own those assets get richer. This is the "wealth effect" economists talk about.

Then the money trickles down. Companies hire more workers. Wages rise. But by the time workers get the new money, the price of housing, food, and energy has already increased. The worker's raise does not keep up with the cost of living. The transfer has already happened.

Why it matters

The Cantillon effect explains why asset owners get richer while wage earners fall behind. It explains why the stock market can hit record highs while main street struggles. It is not a bug in the system. It is a feature of how money is created.

This is why Bitcoin Cash matters. BCH has a fixed supply. No central bank can create new coins. No bank can create deposits out of thin air. The money supply is predictable. The Cantillon effect cannot happen because there is no new money being injected at specific points in the network.

The key insight

The Cantillon effect is not about conspiracy. It is about structure. When money is created by banks and central banks, the first recipients get an advantage. When money has a fixed supply, that advantage disappears. That is one of the reasons Bitcoin Cash exists.

The receipts

Cantillon's insight was published in 1755 in his Essai sur la Nature du Commerce en Général. Modern economists have documented the effect empirically. The Fed's own research shows that monetary policy affects different groups differently. The pattern is real, whether you call it the Cantillon effect or not.
