The ground under your feet

Cash is final, private, and needs no permission. Then a central bank, a gold window, and a digital rail replaced it — and put every dollar on thin ice.

Post 1 of 37 · 8 min read · Oct 22, 2026

Right now, without realizing it, you are walking on thin ice.

Every paycheck that goes into a bank, every payment through Venmo, Cash App, Apple Pay, or a credit card feels safe because it has always worked. But look down: there is nothing solid under you. You don't actually control that money. A middleman does.

That middleman is a system designed to extract value: a central bank that expands the supply, commercial banks that create money from debt, payment processors that take a cut of every transaction, and the custodial apps on your phone — the ones that hold your balance as a promise, not as cash you control. The system was built over a century to make you dependent. Once you see the layers, the ice makes sense.

And middlemen can — and do — track it, take a cut, freeze it, reverse it, leak it, and devalue it over time.

Think about cash for a second. That's solid ground.

When you hand a $10 bill to a neighbor, a farmer, or a kid mowing your lawn, it's done. No permission needed. No 3% to Visa. No bank holding it for "3–5 business days." No company recording what you bought, where you were, and who you were with.

Cash is anonymous without middlemen. The bill doesn't have your name on it. The other person doesn't see your balance or your history. That's not criminal — that's normal life. It's buying a gift without being tracked, donating without getting spammed forever, tipping without a fee, surviving without needing ID, a password, and approval.

That's why people have always needed cash: privacy, freedom, access for everyone, and final payment with no extra fee.

But here's the danger: you can't use cash on the internet.

You can't push a dollar bill through a screen to pay someone in another city, get paid for remote work, buy online, help family overseas, or donate to someone in trouble. Online, cash doesn't exist. So you were forced onto the thin ice — 100% dependent on banks and custodial apps for modern life.

And even before that move online, the dollar itself was never quite the solid ground it seemed. The USD as Military Script makes the case that Federal Reserve Notes are closer to government-issued currency than civilian cash — another layer of ice under your feet.

The ground was not always ice

For most of history, money was a thing you held. A gold coin, a silver dollar, a banknote issued by a local bank. You could lose it, but no one could freeze it with a click. There was no permanent central bank setting the price of credit for the whole country.

Then the United States created one in 1913. The change came after a banking panic and a secret meeting of bankers. What emerged was the Federal Reserve—a central bank with the power to create money, set interest rates, and centralize control over the entire monetary system. The deep dive on the Federal Reserve covers the structure, the power grab, and how it extracted monetary authority from the people.

The Fed was marketed as a way to stop bank panics. What it actually delivered was a small committee with the power to expand the supply of money and set the price of credit for everyone. The daily decisions about how much money exists are made by appointed officials, not by voters or by the market.

The Fed operates behind closed doors. Its real decisions happen in private meetings, not public hearings. The balance sheets it publishes are incomplete — the full ledger of who benefits from every dollar created is hidden. Contrast this with Bitcoin Cash, where every transaction is public, every holder can audit the supply, and full accountability is built into the protocol. With banks, you watch a curated show. With bitcoin, you see everything.
