A small business using BCH for international trade

A Vietnamese coffee exporter sells to a US roaster. The payment settles in minutes, costs almost nothing, and no one needs a traditional bank account or approval.

Post 32 of 36 · 5 min read · Oct 30, 2026

Let's talk numbers. Linh runs a coffee export business in Vietnam. She sells $200,000 worth of beans every year to specialty roasters in the US, Europe, and Japan. When she switched 20% of her payments to Bitcoin Cash, the results were immediate and measurable.

The cost of traditional trade

Before BCH, Linh paid for every international payment. Bank wires cost $25-50 each. Intermediary banks took 1-2% of the transfer. Letters of credit for larger orders cost hundreds of dollars. On a $10,000 shipment, she forfeited $200 in fees alone. On $40,000 of annual volume through traditional channels, that's $800 in direct costs.

But the real cost was time. Wire transfers took 3-5 business days. Funds might be held for review. Letters of credit required weeks of paperwork. Cash flow was unpredictable, and Linh couldn't pay her farmers until the money cleared.

Now 20% of Linh's customers — about $40,000 per year — pay in BCH. The network fee is less than a penny per transaction. Settlement takes minutes, not days. There are no intermediary banks, no letters of credit, no paperwork.

The savings:

  • Direct fees: $800 per year → $0.40 per year (network fees only)
  • Settlement time: 3-5 business days → 10 minutes
  • Cash flow: Predictable, immediate. Linh can pay farmers the same day she ships.
  • Admin time: Hours of paperwork eliminated per month

That's not just a cost reduction. It's a competitive advantage. Linh can offer faster delivery, more flexible payment terms, and better prices to customers who pay in BCH. She's won three new accounts because of it.

Why this matters for small businesses

For large corporations with dedicated treasury departments, the cost of bank wires is a rounding error. For small businesses operating on thin margins, it's a significant expense.

BCH offers a way to settle international payments without the banking system's friction. A small business in Vietnam can sell to a customer in the US, and the payment can settle in minutes with minimal fees. This levels the playing field for small businesses that can't afford the overhead of traditional trade finance.

The payment flow

Linh's invoice structure is the same one any BCH freelancer or merchant uses: quote a price in USD, convert to BCH at the moment of payment, generate a fresh address, and confirm on-chain. The full invoicing flow — quote, invoice, payment, confirmation — is covered in A freelancer paid in BCH. What makes Linh's case different is the scale and the cross-border nature of the trade.

There's no payment processor, no merchant account, no chargeback risk. The payment is final once confirmed. If Linh needs to issue a refund, she sends a new payment — simple, transparent, and under her control.

Accounting and compliance

Linh keeps records in both USD and BCH — the same dual-unit accounting any BCH merchant uses. She logs the invoice amount in USD, the BCH amount received, the exchange rate, and the date. She converts some BCH to Vietnamese dong immediately to cover local expenses and holds some as a hedge against inflation and currency devaluation. Both are deliberate choices, and both work. The important thing is that Linh decides, not a bank or a payment processor.

The business owner's question

"Is this legal?" Linh checked with her accountant and lawyer. In Vietnam, as in most countries, businesses can accept cryptocurrency as payment. The key is to report the income correctly and comply with tax laws. BCH settles directly and cheaply, and the transaction is recorded on a public chain. That's a strong answer for a small business.

Scaling the model

Linh is considering accepting BCH for 100% of her business. The infrastructure is already there. Wallets, exchanges, and merchants exist in many countries. The challenge is adoption and education, not technology.

For small businesses, BCH is not just a payment method — it's a way to participate in global trade without asking permission. The contract is between the buyer and the seller, not between them and a bank.
