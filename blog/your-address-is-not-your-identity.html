Your address is not your identity

A BCH address is a destination, not a name badge. But the ledger is public — so how you use addresses matters more than most people think.

Post 15 of 30 · 3 min read · Oct 22, 2026

Your BCH address looks like a random string: bitcoincash:q.... It is safe to share with someone who is paying you. It is not a password, and it is not your identity. But it is also not invisible.

An address is a destination

An address is a lock. The private key is the key that opens it. When someone pays you, they create an output locked to that address. Your wallet can spend it because it controls the matching key.

Modern BCH addresses use the CashAddr format, usually starting with bitcoincash:. The prefix and checksum make it harder to confuse a BCH address with a Bitcoin address or to lose money to a typo. Older “legacy” addresses still work, but CashAddr is the clearer default. Both formats can point to the same key.

One wallet can have many addresses. Modern wallets are hierarchical deterministic wallets: they derive a new address from the seed for each payment. That is not just a cosmetic feature. It is a privacy tool.

Why address reuse leaks

The BCH ledger is public. If you post one address in your social profile and use it for every payment, anyone can see every transaction to that address. They can see the amounts, the timing, and the other addresses those payments interact with. They may not know your name, but they can build a profile.

Chain analysis is the business of clustering addresses and linking them to real-world identities. Exchanges that require identity checks create a natural link between a person and a withdrawal address. From there, analysts can follow the money through the public graph. None of this requires breaking cryptography. It uses the transparency of the ledger and the carelessness of address reuse.

Privacy is not anonymity

BCH is pseudonymous, not anonymous. A fresh address per payment makes linking harder, but it does not make you invisible. On-ramps, off-ramps, IP addresses, and merchant records can all create links. Privacy is a practice, not a switch.

Good address hygiene

  • Use a fresh address for each invoice or payment. Let the wallet generate it. Do not copy the same address into every profile.
  • Do not publish a savings address. A public “donate to me” address is a public transaction history. If you need a public donation address, understand that trade-off.
  • Check the prefix and a few characters. CashAddr checksums catch many typos, but clipboard malware and lookalike addresses are a different problem. Verify the first and last characters with the sender.
  • Keep spending and savings separate. A phone wallet with a small balance limits what a compromised app can take. A cold wallet for savings limits what a malicious website can reach.
  • Remember the edges. Buying BCH with a bank transfer or card usually requires identity verification. That record exists outside the chain and can be linked to your wallet.

The address is not the person

It is tempting to treat a BCH address as an account number and post it everywhere. That is the old banking habit again. On a transparent ledger, an address is better understood as a one-time destination. You can reuse it, but you are choosing to publish a history.

Privacy is not about having something to hide. It is about not handing out a permanent financial diary by default. A new address costs nothing. Reusing one leaks for free.
