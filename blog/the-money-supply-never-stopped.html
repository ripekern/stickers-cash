The money supply never stopped

M2 did shrink in 2022–23. Then it reversed. By August 2026 the money supply hit a record $23.34 trillion — here is the cycle in one page.

Post 4 of 30 · 2 min read · Oct 22, 2026

It is easy to believe the money supply is shrinking. For a couple of years, it was. The mistake is assuming that is still true.

M2 is a broad measure of money — cash, checking and savings deposits, money-market funds, and small time deposits. It did contract hard in 2022 and 2023, when the Federal Reserve raised rates and shrank its balance sheet to fight inflation. But that trend reversed. According to the Fed's H.6 release, M2 climbed steadily through 2025 and 2026.

Time periodM2 trendPrimary driver
2020 – 2021📈 Explosive expansionPandemic stimulus and quantitative easing
2022 – 2023📉 Historic contractionRapid rate hikes and balance-sheet shrinkage to fight inflation
2024 – 2026📈 Renewed expansionBank deposits stabilizing, and a shift back toward monetary liquidity expansion

The temporary 2022–2023 decline was the first sustained drop in the modern M2 series since the Great Depression. That phase is over. The total money supply has fully surpassed its previous 2022 peak.
