Tokens on the cash layer

CashTokens add divisible tokens and NFTs to BCH itself — not to a sidechain, not to a separate ledger. The fees are still paid in BCH.

Post 26 of 36 · 3 min read · Oct 22, 2026

Bitcoin Cash has a built-in token system called CashTokens. It was added in May 2023, and it lives right on the BCH network — no sidechain, no bridge needed. The same computers that check regular BCH payments also check token transfers, and fees are still paid in BCH.

Two kinds of token

CashTokens gives you two tools. A fungible token is divisible and interchangeable — like points in a loyalty program or a community currency. An NFT is indivisible and carries a small piece of data — like a ticket, a receipt, or a membership card.

Every token belongs to a family, identified by a unique ID tied to the transaction that created it. The network itself enforces the rules: how many tokens exist, how they move, and who controls them. You do not have to trust a company's database — the network checks everything.

Tokens travel alongside BCH in the same transaction. A single payment can move coins, fungible tokens, and an NFT all at once. That makes things easy: a shop can require payment in BCH and a token transfer in the same step.

No extra fee token to buy

On some networks, using an app requires you to buy a second kind of token just to pay for the computer time. On BCH, the fee is just the normal BCH network fee. A token transfer costs a fraction of a cent, and there is nothing extra to buy before you can use the network.

That is a meaningful UX difference. A community currency, a loyalty program, or a ticket system does not need to teach users two assets. They hold BCH for fees and the token for the application. The wallet can show both, and the transaction can move both at once.

What tokens can represent

A fungible token can stand in for a community currency, a voucher, a share in a project, a loyalty balance, or a digital dollar issued by a trusted party. An NFT can be a ticket, a membership, a receipt, a collectible, or a piece of information that a rule can check.

CashTokens make the token itself verifiable: the family, the supply, and the transfer rules are all checked by the network. The issuer's promise is separate. A voucher is useful because a business honors it; a digital dollar is useful because an issuer redeems it. CashTokens give that promise a transparent, on-chain footprint. Choose issuers with a track record, and the token layer does the rest.

Pick a wallet that shows tokens

CashTokens are built in, and modern BCH wallets support them. A good wallet shows your token balances right next to your BCH and builds the right transaction for you. Use a wallet that shows tokens clearly, and always check what you are sending. The ecosystem already has wallets, explorers, and tools for tokens — and it is growing.

Why built-in matters

You could build tokens on top of any chain using add-on layers. CashTokens takes a different route: put the rules directly into the network. Every computer running BCH checks the rules. There is no extra service you have to trust.

That is the BCH pattern again. Keep the core small, but make the core actually enforce the things people need. Tokens are useful. Making them built-in makes them cheaper, safer, and easier for everyone to use. The category IDs and consensus rules show how it all fits together at the protocol level.

NFTs are one form of native token — and they are more interesting than a link to a JPEG. NFTs with the receipt built in shows how a BCH NFT carries an on-chain commitment that contracts can verify.
