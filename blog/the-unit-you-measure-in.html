The unit you measure in

BCH is scarce, spendable, and priced in an open market. The unit you measure in changes what you notice — and the coin count is the number that stays yours.

Post 17 of 30 · 3 min read · Oct 22, 2026

Open a BCH price chart and you see two things: a market quote in dollars and a network that settles in seconds. The quote moves with demand. The coin count does not. Shift the unit, and the picture becomes clearer.

Two monies, one exchange rate

The price of BCH in dollars moves with global demand. The number of coins in your wallet does not. Ten BCH is still ten BCH. That is the first thing to notice: the protocol gives you a fixed supply, and the market gives you a daily quote.

The dollar is not a fixed ruler either. It buys less over time as the money supply expands. BCH gives you a unit that cannot be diluted, and a network that can move it anywhere for a fraction of a cent.

The jobs of money

Money serves as a medium of exchange, a unit of account, and a store of value. BCH is designed to be excellent at exchange and scarce as a store of value. Its large blocks keep settlement cheap, and its 21-million-coin cap keeps the supply fixed. That combination is the point.

As more people use BCH, more of them will price goods directly in it. That is how a new unit of account grows: first people hold it, then they spend it, then they quote prices in it. BCH is built for all three steps.

A fixed supply is a fixed supply

Scarcity is a protocol rule, not a daily quote. Demand can change the exchange rate, but it cannot change the 21-million-coin cap. Every BCH is divisible into 100 million satoshis, so you can spend a tiny fraction without touching the rest of your savings.

That is a different kind of money from a bank balance that can be expanded at will. The supply schedule is transparent, auditable, and enforced by every full node.

A growing market

BCH trades 24/7 on a global market. That liquidity is a strength: you can move value when you choose, anywhere in the world. The market is open, competitive, and increasingly connected to real usage. As the network grows, the quote becomes a smaller part of the story.

The better question is not what the dollar number did today. It is what the network can do: settle a payment in seconds, for a fraction of a cent, without a bank in the middle.

Spending and savings

A simple setup keeps both jobs separate. Use a phone wallet with a small spendable balance for everyday purchases. Keep longer-term savings in a hardware wallet. If you need cash for a repair, spend a fraction of a coin and leave the rest untouched.

That is a resilient way to use BCH. The spending wallet handles the day-to-day, and the savings wallet holds the part you do not touch. Both are yours, and both are under your control.

BCH has a fixed supply and a growing network. Measure in coins, and the daily quote becomes background noise. The number that matters is how many coins you hold — and what they can do.
