The real-return trap

A 5% CD feels safe until you subtract money-supply growth. The math of “safe” savings — and why so many people still choose it.

Post 7 of 30 · 3 min read · Oct 22, 2026

Now the trap closes.

If the M2 money supply doubles and the amount of goods and services in the economy stays the same, prices will eventually double — and your purchasing power drops by exactly 50%. Think of the economy as an auction. If the number of items for sale stays the same but everyone suddenly has twice as much cash, they bid up the price of everything.

If M2 goes from $23 trillion to $46 trillion, a $10,000 home repair doesn't fundamentally change in value. It eventually costs $20,000, because each individual dollar is worth less.

The 5% illusion

This is why earning 5% on digital USD can be a financial illusion. What matters is your real rate of return: your interest rate minus the rate of monetary expansion.

OptionRates low (2%)Rates high (5.25%)
💰 Leave $10,000 in the bank You earn $200 a year. The money feels like it is rotting, so you want to spend it. You earn a guaranteed $525 a year in risk-free digital cash. The money feels like an active asset.
🛠️ Spend $10,000 on a new roof The roof gets fixed, and your bank account wasn't earning anything anyway. Easy choice. Fixing the roof now "costs" the $10,000 plus the $525 in interest you would have earned.

Put $100,000 into a CD at 5% and after a year you have $105,000. You feel richer. But if M2 grew 5.6% over the same year, the overall pool of dollars expanded faster than your pile. You have more dollars and slightly less purchasing power. That is the hidden tax: nominal gain, real loss.

Higher rates deliberately shift the public mood from consumption to preservation. The Fed makes borrowing expensive and saving lucrative so people stop spending. The drop in home maintenance is that policy working in the real world — even if it means living with leaky faucets and aging roofs to protect a digital balance sheet.

Why people save anyway

  1. Nominal loss aversion. People hate watching their bank balance fall today, even while inflation erodes it quietly over time.
  2. Immediate bills beat future loss. You can't pay next month's mortgage, insurance, or grocery bill with "future purchasing power." People hoard cash because they need liquidity now.
  3. The prey mentality. In an uncertain economy, cash is an emergency shield. People accept a slow burn from inflation to avoid the catastrophic risk of running out of money during a crisis.

So holding digital USD buys short-term survival security at the cost of long-term wealth. That is the trade millions are making without ever naming it.

You are not choosing between safety and risk. You are choosing which risk to carry.
