What to do with your first BCH

You have BCH in your wallet. Now what? The simplest first spend, how to think about your stack, and why buying a sticker might be the best move you make today.

Post 23 of 37 · 3 min read · Nov 6, 2026

You have coins in your wallet. The hardest part is over. Now the question is: do you hold, trade, or spend? The answer is: spend a little, hold the rest.

Why your first spend matters

Holding is easy. Spending is the real test. When you send BCH to someone — a friend, a merchant, a donation — you are doing something that most BCH holders have never done. You are using it as money.

That first spend teaches you more than reading ten posts. You learn how fees work (they are almost nothing). You learn how fast it is (seconds). You learn that it is final (no chargebacks, no reversals). And you learn that you can do it without permission.

The simplest first spend

Here is the easiest way to make your first transaction:

  1. Buy a sticker. Go to stickers.cash. Pick a design. Check out with BCH. The whole process takes about two minutes, and you get something physical in the mail. You just used internet cash.
  2. Tip the guide. If this guide was useful, send a small tip to the BCH address on the donate page. Even $0.50 teaches you how donations work.
  3. Send to a friend. If someone you know has a wallet, send them $1. They will either be confused or impressed. Either way, you just moved money peer-to-peer.

Any of these works. The point is not the amount. The point is the action. You moved from "I have BCH" to "I used BCH." That is a real shift.

How to think about your stack

Once you have spent a little, the rest is yours to hold. Here is a useful mindset:

  • Do not think in dollars. The dollar price of BCH moves. Your coin count does not. If you own 0.1 BCH today, you will own 0.1 BCH next year (minus whatever you spend). That number is what stays yours.
  • Start small, add slowly. You do not need to buy a lot. $20 a month, dollar-cost averaged, builds a stack without timing the market.
  • Self-custody matters. If your coins are on an exchange, you do not really own them. Move them to your wallet. Practice the backup. Know that you can recover your phrase.

What to do next

Once you have made your first spend and your coins are in your wallet, you are in the same position as every other BCH holder. You have internet cash. You control it. You can spend it anywhere it is accepted, and more places accept it every day.

The next posts show you what that looks like in practice. A day in the life with BCH walks through a regular day — coffee, rent, sending money to family — and shows how BCH fits into each one.
