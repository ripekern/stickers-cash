The roof you didn't fix

Repairs cost more, borrowing costs more, and cash pays 5%. So homeowners are living with leaky faucets — and turning $1 of deferred work into $4 of damage.

Post 6 of 10 · 3 min read · Oct 22, 2026

This is where macro numbers become a kitchen-table decision. Homeowners are putting off necessary maintenance because labor costs, materials, and interest rates have turned basic upkeep into a financial strain. An estimated 71% of homeowners delayed at least one critical repair over the past year. Half report needing essential repairs right now that they simply cannot afford.

1. Repairs got expensive

The price of maintaining a home has surged faster than general inflation. The median cost of a home improvement project jumped 76.1% over the last decade, even after adjusting for inflation. Contractor labor rates are high, and supply pressure plus tariffs keep basic materials elevated.

2. Borrowing got expensive

When a roof, foundation, or HVAC unit fails, most homeowners don't have $5,000–$10,000 in cash sitting around. Borrowing has become exceptionally expensive: home equity loan rates average around 7.75%, and HELOCs hover around 7.3%. So people max out credit cards or avoid secured home loans entirely, choosing instead to wait out the economy.

3. Golden handcuffs and aging homes

Millions of homeowners locked in historically low mortgage rates — under 3% or 4% — during the pandemic. Moving or upgrading is financially impossible for many, so they stay in their current homes far longer than planned. As those homes age, major systems deteriorate at the same time, leaving owners facing several expensive repairs at once.

4. Fear of insurance consequences

Nearly 1 in 4 homeowners admit they have skipped filing necessary home insurance claims. In a volatile property insurance market, they fear a claim will trigger an inspection, force unaffordable mandated upgrades, or cause their insurer to drop coverage entirely.

The risk of waiting

Deferred maintenance behaves like high-interest debt. Industry data shows every $1 of repair work put off today tends to scale into $4 or more in damage later, as small leaks and minor structural issues become major emergencies.

And the cost of waiting is rising, because rates are not falling. The Federal Reserve raised its benchmark rate by 0.25% in September 2026, to a target range of 3.75%–4.00% — the first hike since 2023. The Fed has warned that persistent core inflation means more hikes are likely, and most major economists expect another 25 basis points in December.

Consumer rates follow. The average 30-year fixed mortgage climbed back to 7.40%–7.47%, up from a 2026 low of 6.01% in February. Fannie Mae expects mortgage rates to stay near the mid-7.50% range through 2027. Credit card and auto loan rates are creeping higher too. The one silver lining: if you are saving cash, top-tier short-term CDs pay up to 5.25%.
