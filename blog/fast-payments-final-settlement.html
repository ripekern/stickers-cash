Fast payments, final settlement

A BCH payment reaches the receiver in seconds and settles in a block. That is not a delay — it is certainty.

Post 13 of 30 · 4 min read · Oct 22, 2026

A BCH payment reaches the receiver in seconds. The message travels across the network, the shop's wallet sees it, and the customer gets a confirmation on their screen. Settlement is the second step: the transaction is included in a block and becomes final. That is not a delay. It is certainty.

Two kinds of instant

Traditional card payments blur two different events. The authorization feels instant, while the actual settlement between banks can take days. BCH makes both steps visible. The payment is broadcast instantly, and the block gives it final settlement.

That clarity is useful. A merchant can see the payment arrive and can decide, with a clear policy, when to hand over the goods. For a small in-person purchase, the broadcast is enough. For a larger or remote order, a confirmation gives full certainty.

Blocks are batches

BCH collects valid transactions into a block and adds that block to the chain. The network targets one block every ten minutes. That target is an average, not a schedule; a block can arrive sooner. The ten-minute cadence is the heartbeat of a global settlement network, and it gives nodes time to propagate transactions and agree on the chain.

BCH's capacity model keeps the queue clear. A payment does not wait because the network is congested. It waits for the next block, and blocks have room for the traffic. That is the difference between a heartbeat and a bottleneck.

Instant for small purchases

For a four-dollar sticker, the shop can hand over the goods as soon as the payment appears. The customer is standing there, the amount is small, and the transaction is visible. This is one of the practical advantages of cheap, fast BCH payments: the checkout flow feels as quick as cash, and the shop keeps the sale moving.

BCH also gives merchants tools that make instant acceptance even more robust. For everyday retail, the combination of instant visibility and a small ticket size makes this a smooth, confident experience.

Confirmations build certainty

Once a transaction is in a block, it has one confirmation. Each additional block makes the settlement more final. A four-dollar sticker does not need the same assurance as a car, and the protocol gives the merchant a clear scale: accept instantly for small in-person sales, wait for one or more confirmations for larger or remote orders.

That is not a compromise. It is a merchant-friendly policy that matches the value of the sale. BCH gives the shop both speed and finality, and the shop chooses the point on the scale that fits its business.

Compare it with a card

A card authorization is instant, but the money can take days to settle between banks, and a chargeback can reverse the payment weeks later. BCH settles directly and finally. The merchant gets certainty, and a refund is a deliberate new payment that is simple to send and easy to record.

For a shop, that is a stronger position. The payment is not a promise from an issuer. It is a transaction on a public chain, verified by the network and final once confirmed.

Why ten minutes?

Block time is a security parameter. It gives the network time to propagate blocks and agree on the chain. BCH pairs that cadence with large, adaptive blocks, so capacity is not the limiting factor for ordinary payments. The result is a network that is fast where it matters and final where it counts.

The practical rule is simple: small and local, accept instantly; larger or remote, wait for a confirmation. BCH gives you instant visibility and final settlement. That combination is what a payment network should feel like.
