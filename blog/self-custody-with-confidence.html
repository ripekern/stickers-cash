Self-custody with confidence

Self-custody is not a test of bravery. It is a tested backup, a simple routine, and the freedom to hold your own money.

Post 16 of 30 · 3 min read · Oct 22, 2026

Self-custody is simpler than it sounds. It is a short checklist: create a wallet, write down the seed, test the restore, and keep the seed private. That is it.

The reward is direct ownership. The BCH in your wallet is yours. No bank can freeze it, no platform can close your account, and no intermediary has to approve a payment. That is the freedom self-custody gives you.

Start with a practice wallet

Create a wallet with a small amount of BCH. Write down the seed phrase. Send a payment to yourself. Restore the wallet on a second device. Delete the app and restore it again. Do this while the amount is small, so the focus is on the steps rather than the balance.

The first goal is familiarity. You want to know what the seed does, what happens when you restore, and what a transaction looks like. A few minutes of practice makes the whole flow feel normal.

Back up the seed properly

The seed phrase is the master backup. Write it on paper or stamp it into metal. Keep it offline. Do not photograph it, email it, save it in a password manager, or store it in cloud notes. Those are convenient ways to create a second copy that someone else can reach.

Keep more than one copy in more than one place, so a fire or flood does not erase your money. If you use an optional passphrase, back it up separately from the seed. A passphrase adds security, and a separate backup keeps the restore path clear.

The backup test

A tested backup is a proven backup. Restore it on a spare device before the wallet holds anything important. If the balance comes back, you have confirmation that your setup works.

A simple, strong setup

Most people do not need a complicated setup. A phone wallet with a small spending balance and a hardware wallet for savings covers both jobs. The phone wallet is for everyday purchases; the hardware wallet keeps the long-term balance offline. That is a clean boundary, and it is easy to maintain.

Multisignature and inheritance plans are useful when the amount justifies them. Start simple, learn the flow, and add complexity only when it serves a clear purpose. A setup you understand is stronger than an elaborate one you do not.

The one rule

No legitimate support agent, wallet update, giveaway, or exchange needs your seed phrase. Keep it private, and you keep control. A wallet you chose, on a device you control, is the right place to enter it.

Self-custody is a skill, and it is simpler than it looks. Once your backup is tested, you can hold BCH with the same confidence you have in the cash in your pocket — only this money is yours in a way a bank balance never is.
