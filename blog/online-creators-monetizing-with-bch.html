Online creators monetizing with BCH

A musician in Brazil sells directly to fans worldwide. The payments settle in minutes, cost pennies, and no platform takes a cut.

Post 35 of 37 · 4 min read · Oct 30, 2026

Rafael is an independent musician in São Paulo. He makes electronic music and sells it directly to fans through his website. He sells digital downloads, vinyl, and merchandise. He accepts Bitcoin Cash.

When a fan in Germany buys an album for $10, the payment settles in minutes and costs a fraction of a cent in network fees. Rafael keeps the full amount — no platform takes 30%, no payment processor holds the funds, and no currency conversion eats into the sale.

The old way

Most independent creators use platforms like Bandcamp, Patreon, or Gumroad to sell their work. These platforms are useful — they handle hosting, payment processing, and delivery — but they take a significant cut.

Bandcamp takes 15% of digital sales (or more during non-free download days). Patreon takes 5% to 12% depending on the plan. Gumroad takes 10% of every sale. For a $10 album, the creator might receive $8.50 to $9.50, with the rest going to the platform.

For international sales, the fees are worse. Payment processors add currency conversion markups, and the platforms may charge additional fees for cross-border transactions. A fan in Japan buying from a Brazilian creator might pay more, and the creator receives less.

The BCH way

Rafael's website has a simple checkout flow. The fan selects an album, enters their email, and chooses to pay with BCH. The site calculates the BCH amount from the current exchange rate, generates a fresh address, and displays a QR code. The fan scans the QR with their wallet, sends the payment, and receives a download link.

The payment settles in minutes, costs a fraction of a cent, and Rafael receives the full amount. There's no platform fee, no payment processor cut, and no currency conversion markup. The fan pays $10, Rafael receives $10 (minus a fraction of a cent in network fees).

Why this matters for creators

For creators operating on thin margins, every percentage point matters. A 15% platform fee on a $10 album is $1.50 — money that could go to equipment, studio time, or the next project. Over hundreds of sales, those fees add up.

BCH offers a way to sell directly to fans without the middleman. The creator controls the checkout flow, the pricing, and the relationship with the audience. The fan gets a direct connection to the creator, not a transaction mediated by a platform.

Building the flow

Rafael's website is a static HTML site with a small backend that generates BCH addresses and delivers downloads. The architecture is similar to stickers.cash:

  • Static site: The website is hosted on GitHub Pages or a similar static host. Product pages display albums, descriptions, and prices.
  • Address derivation: A Cloudflare Worker (or similar) generates a fresh BCH address for each order. The worker uses an xpub to derive addresses without exposing private keys.
  • Delivery: After payment, the site sends a download link to the fan's email. The link is time-limited and unique to the order.
  • Accounting: Rafael logs each sale: the USD price, the BCH amount, the exchange rate, and the date. He reports the USD value for tax purposes.

The infrastructure is simple, cheap, and under Rafael's control. He doesn't need a platform to sell his music, and he doesn't need to share his revenue.

Direct-to-fan relationships

Platforms like Bandcamp and Patreon are useful for discovery — they help creators find new fans. But they also mediate the relationship. The fan interacts with the platform, not the creator. The platform owns the data, the email list, and the transaction history.

When Rafael sells directly, he owns the relationship. He has the fan's email, he knows what they bought, and he can communicate with them directly. He can announce new releases, offer exclusive content, and build a community around his work.

This is not just about money — it's about ownership and control. The creator decides how to sell, who to sell to, and how to use the revenue. The platform is not in the middle.

The creator's question

"Can I reach fans without a platform?" Platforms help with discovery, but they also take a cut and mediate the relationship. BCH lets creators sell directly, keep the full revenue, and own the fan relationship. Discovery is still a challenge, but the monetization is under the creator's control.

Privacy for fans

Fans who want to support a creator anonymously can send BCH without revealing their identity. The transaction is public on the blockchain, but the fan's name is not attached to it. This is useful for fans who want to support a creator without public recognition.

Fans who want to be recognized can provide their email and name, and the creator can acknowledge them publicly. The choice is the fan's, not the platform's.

Scaling the model

Rafael is not unique. Independent musicians, writers, artists, and developers all face the same friction: high platform fees, limited control, and mediated relationships. Bitcoin Cash offers a way to sell directly to audiences without those costs.

The infrastructure is already there. Wallets, exchanges, and payment tools exist. The challenge is adoption and education, not technology. Creators who adopt BCH early can build direct relationships with their audiences and keep the full revenue from their work.

For creators, BCH is not just a payment method — it's a way to monetize without asking permission. The relationship is between the creator and the fan, not between them and a platform.
