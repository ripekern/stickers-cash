Cash, rebuilt for the internet

Bitcoin Cash is what cash looks like when it has to travel through a screen: peer-to-peer, self-custodied, and final in seconds.

Post 3 of 30 · 3 min read · Oct 22, 2026

Bitcoin — specifically Bitcoin Cash (BCH) — is cash rebuilt for the internet so you can get off that ice now.

It works just like handing someone a bill, but online. You hold it yourself in your own wallet, like cash in your pocket. You send it directly peer-to-peer to anyone, anywhere, in seconds. No bank, no Visa, no PayPal in the middle to watch, charge, block, or reverse it.

No ID to create a wallet. Less than a penny in fees, even for $1,000 across the world. Final settlement, not a promise. Money that is actually yours.

What changes when money is a bearer asset

The difference is not just speed or fees. It is who holds the claim.

QuestionDigital dollarBitcoin Cash
Who holds it?A bank or payment app holds the balance. You hold a promise.You hold the key. The coin is on a public ledger.
Who can create more?The central bank and commercial banks, within policy and capital rules.No one. The 21 million supply cap is part of the protocol.
Who can freeze it?The institution, platform, or government that controls the account.No bank, platform, or government can freeze the coin without your keys.
Who pays for expansion?Everyone holding the currency, through higher prices.No one. The supply does not expand.
How does payment settle?Through banks and processors, with reversibility and fees.Peer-to-peer in seconds, final on-chain, for less than a cent.

That is the difference between a promise and a bearer asset. A dollar in a bank is a liability of the bank. A BCH coin is not anyone's liability. It is a digital object that you control with a key, and the network checks the signature without asking a bank for permission.

This is not about hating banks

Banks do useful things. They provide credit, custody, fraud protection, and payment infrastructure. The point is not that banking should disappear. The point is that you should have a choice that does not require permission.

Cash gave people an exit from the banking system. You could keep a week's wages in your pocket and no one could freeze it. BCH gives the internet an exit. If you hold the keys, no bank can lend out your balance, no processor can reverse your payment, and no committee can expand the supply.

That is the whole promise: not a different app, but a different property right. You own the money directly, the way you own a dollar bill.

The first step

You do not have to move your whole life today. Start with a small amount, a non-custodial wallet, and a written backup of the seed phrase. Send a small payment. Check the address. Learn the flow before the balance matters.

Post 10 covers the practical start in detail. The point here is simpler: the exit exists. You are not trapped on the ice.

People who switch say the same thing: "I'm so thankful I learned this. How did I get by without it?" They sleep better knowing rent money can't be frozen, their donations can't be censored, their small business can't be de-platformed, and their savings aren't melting on someone else's ledger.

You don't have to wait for the ice to crack to step onto solid ground.

Part one is about freedom. But freedom alone rarely changes behavior. What makes the ice urgent is quieter: the steady expansion of the money supply, and what it does to anyone trying to save.
