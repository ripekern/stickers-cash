The network that passed the test

Bitcoin Cash has been deliberately flooded under real load. It kept clearing blocks and keeping fees low — a demonstration of what on-chain capacity is for.

Post 19 of 36 · 3 min read · Oct 22, 2026

Scaling claims are easy to make. Anyone can say their network will handle the world's payments. A stress test is harder to fake: you flood the network, watch the blocks, watch the fees, and see whether the thing actually clears.

September 2018: 2.1 million transactions

In September 2018, the BCH community ran a coordinated stress test on the mainnet. The goal was to push millions of minimum-fee transactions through the network in 24 hours and see how it performed. More than 2.1 million transactions were processed.

The result was steady fees. Reports at the time showed average fees staying around a fraction of a cent, actually dipping from roughly $0.002 to $0.0017. Blocks kept being mined. The chain kept moving. The queue stayed clear.

That test mattered because it was a deliberate attempt to fill the network with low-value traffic. The network was asked to do the thing big blocks are supposed to do: carry volume without making every payment expensive. It did.

2021: the load was real

In early 2021, BCH processed more than 300,000 transactions in a day, and miners produced multi-megabyte blocks, including a 4.7 MB block with nearly 15,000 transactions. Fees stayed around a fraction of a cent.

That is evidence that larger blocks work in the wild. Miners included them. Nodes validated them. The network settled them. The capacity was used by real users, not only in a lab.

2024: testing the limit itself

The adaptive block limit added a new question: what happens when demand keeps rising for years? The answer was modeled before it was needed. Stress-test simulations run sustained demand through the algorithm and watch the limit rise fast enough to keep blocks from staying full.

The goal of the simulation is specific: keep the waiting area clear and fees low as usage grows. The limit moves gradually, so the network gets a smooth adjustment. The 32 MB floor remains underneath.

The simulation proves the mechanism responds. That is exactly what a stress test is for.

What the tests prove

They prove that BCH can process large blocks, that miners will mine them, that nodes will validate them, and that fees stay low when the network is busy. They prove that a payment network can be designed to clear a queue rather than ration it.

That is the engineering question, and it has been answered. BCH's rails carry volume without collapsing into a fee market. The capacity argument is settled.

The point of the test

A payment network is proven by what happens when traffic arrives. BCH has been flooded on purpose, has carried real bursts of usage, and has modeled sustained demand through its adaptive limit. In each case, the design did what it was supposed to do: keep blocks moving and keep fees low.

The capacity argument is no longer a hypothesis. The network clears the queue, and it is ready for the world to fill it.

And that capacity unlocks something most people don't expect: programmable money. Money that can run rules shows how every BCH coin is locked by a small program — and what that changes.
